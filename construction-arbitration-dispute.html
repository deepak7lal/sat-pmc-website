Start with the contract and the project record

Construction disputes can involve overlapping questions about scope, progress, instructions and cost. A technical review begins by understanding the relevant contract and the records that describe the work. SAT PMC's support brings planning, contracts and quantity surveying perspectives together so that technical and commercial issues can be examined coherently.

Forensic delay analysis

Delay analysis considers the programme and what occurred during delivery. Baseline schedules, updates, progress records, instructions and correspondence may all be relevant. The method and extent of the review depend on the question being addressed and the information available. Our planning capability also includes delay assessment, mitigation and extension of time claim support.

Quantum assessment and claim support

A claim may require assessment of the quantity or value of a change or an asserted impact. Quantity surveying, estimates, material records and cost information can contribute to that review. SAT PMC supports claim preparation and defence, as well as the technical work needed for arbitration support. The appointment should identify the matters to be reviewed and the required outputs.

Define the review before assembling the submission

For an initial discussion, share the project type, current stage, broad nature of the issue and the support sought. The availability of the contract, programmes, instructions, correspondence and cost records can then be considered. A clear scope helps the team focus the review on the specific delay or commercial questions that need to be addressed.

Common questions

Do you support both claim preparation and defence?+

Yes. Our capability includes technical support for claim preparation and defence. The scope is agreed after understanding the issue, the contract and the available project records.

What is useful for the first discussion?+

A brief description of the project and issue, the contract type, key dates, current status and an outline of the available programme and cost records will help define the next step.
